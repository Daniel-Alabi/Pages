Concept Design Dossier · Rev A

ANAX-4 A four-station tilt-proprotor air taxi derived from Anax junius, the green darner

The dragonfly is the best hovering, intercepting, and long-migrating flyer that evolution has produced. It is also the wrong size, the wrong Reynolds number, and the wrong actuator technology for carrying four people across a city. This dossier separates what the dragonfly does from how the dragonfly does it — and copies only the first.

Method: functional biomimicry Class: powered-lift, 4 pax Status: concept, pre-preliminary

The design question

Which of the dragonfly's solutions survive a jump of five orders of magnitude in mass and three orders in Reynolds number — and which are artefacts of being small?

01 / BIOLOGY

What the dragonfly actually does

Eight mechanisms are worth studying. Each card names the biological function first, then the mechanism. The pterostigma mark in the corner records how confident the engineering transfer is — not how interesting the biology is.

Four independently driven wings

direct flight musculature

Unlike most insects, Odonata attach muscle directly to each wing base. Amplitude, frequency and stroke plane are set per wing. The result is an over-actuated flyer that can hover, fly backwards, and sideslip without reorienting the body.

Fore–hind phase modulation

counter-stroking ↔ in-phase

Roughly 180° out of phase for efficient cruise and hover — the hindwing works in favourable forewing downwash. Shifted toward in-phase for maximum instantaneous force during takeoff and attack. Phase, not throttle, is the primary power lever.

Leading-edge vortex capture

unsteady high-lift

A stable LEV sits over the wing through the stroke, producing lift coefficients two to four times quasi-steady prediction. This is why insects hover on so little wing area.

Corrugated wing section

pleated, not streamlined

The wing is a zig-zag in cross-section. Vortices park in the valleys and act as a virtual smooth surface, while the pleats deliver extraordinary spanwise and torsional stiffness for almost no mass.

Pterostigma

outboard pigmented mass

A small heavy cell near the leading edge, outboard. It is a passive aeroelastic device: it shifts the wing's mass axis forward and raises the flutter onset speed, letting the wing be lighter than a flutter-free wing otherwise could be.

Long slender abdomen

moment arm + radiator

Carries almost no load. It buys large pitch and yaw moment arms for negligible mass, shifts the CG in flight, and radiates thoracic heat during sustained flight.

Interception guidance

TSDNs, ~95% capture rate

Target-selective descending neurons drive a predictive interception course — the animal steers to a future intercept point, holding the target on a constant bearing. This is proportional navigation, implemented in about sixteen neurons per side.

Dual attitude sensing

compound eyes + ocelli triad

~30,000 ommatidia for motion and target detection; three simple ocelli providing a fast, coarse, horizon-referenced attitude signal on a separate pathway. Two dissimilar channels, different latencies, different failure modes.

03 / THE SCALING FILTER

Why the flapping has to go

This is the section that decides the whole aircraft. Three independent physical arguments each kill flapping flight at passenger scale. Any one of them would be sufficient.

Argument 1 — Reynolds number

A green darner cruises with a chord Reynolds number around 2,000–10,000. In that regime, boundary layers separate readily, conventional airfoils perform badly, and unsteady vortex capture is genuinely the best available strategy. A 2.3 t vehicle at 67 m/s with a 1.1 m chord operates near Re ≈ 5 × 10⁶.

# Chord Reynolds number, ANAX-4 cruise Re = ρ·V·c / μ = (1.225 × 67 × 1.10) / 1.81e-5 = 4.99 × 10⁶ — dragonfly: ~5 × 10³ # The regime the LEV advantage lives in ends around Re ~ 1e4. # At 5e6 an attached-flow airfoil already reaches L/D > 60 in 2-D. Verdict: unsteady high-lift solves a problem we do not have.

Argument 2 — wing inertia and flapping frequency

Flapping frequency scales roughly as f ∝ m^(-1/3) across flying animals. Extrapolating from a 0.5 g dragonfly at ~35 Hz gives a laughable answer at 2,300 kg — and the reciprocating inertial loads make it worse than laughable.

# Greenewalt-type frequency scaling f₂ = f₁ · (m₁/m₂)^(1/3) = 35 · (0.0005 / 2300)^(1/3) = 35 · (2.17e-7)^(1/3) = 35 × 0.0060 = 0.21 Hz — one stroke every 4.8 seconds # Peak inertial torque of a reciprocating wing, half-amplitude θ₀: T_peak = I · θ₀ · (2πf)² # For a 5 m, 90 kg wing: I ≈ 750 kg·m², θ₀ = 0.6 rad # Even at 0.21 Hz this is ~790 N·m of pure inertial reversal, # twice per cycle, for the entire life of the airframe. Verdict: a fatigue-driven structure with no aerodynamic payoff.
The scale trap

At 0.21 Hz there is no unsteady aerodynamics left. The reduced frequency k = πfc/V collapses to ~0.005, which is quasi-steady flow by any definition. You would carry all of flapping's structural cost and receive none of its aerodynamic benefit. Flapping is not a scaled-down version of a good idea; it is a good idea that only exists at small scale.

Argument 3 — certification and fatigue

A rotor is a steady-load device with well-understood fatigue methodology and sixty years of certification precedent. A flapping wing is a fully reversing-load primary structure with no certification basis, no established damage-tolerance approach, and no inspection standard. Under a 10⁻⁹ target this is not a schedule risk — it is a programme-ending one.

04 / CONCEPT SPACE

Four configurations, one survivor

With flapping eliminated, the remaining question is how to arrange four force-producing stations. Candidates were scored against the requirement set, weighted by which requirements are hard constraints.

The decision that shaped the aircraft

Config C is the most literal dragonfly translation — four stations, four rotors, tandem. It was rejected on a single number: losing one of four rotors costs 25% of thrust and generates an unrecoverable rolling and pitching moment at low airspeed. Sizing for that case demands roughly 1.6× thrust margin across the whole system.

Config D keeps the four-station topology — the part that came from the dragonfly — and puts a contra-rotating coaxial pair at each station. One motor out now costs 12.5% at one station, with near-zero net moment change. The penalty is coaxial interference (~14% on induced power) and a new acoustic interaction tone. Both penalties are then attacked using dragonfly phase control — see §08.

Binding constraint

The battery is 29% of MTOW and the empty-weight fraction is 76%. Range is not limited by aerodynamics, propulsion or architecture — it is limited entirely by pack-level specific energy. At 265 Wh/kg the aircraft closes at 90 km. At 220 Wh/kg it closes at roughly 55 km and the business case fails. At 350 Wh/kg it reaches ~150 km.

This is why the range requirement in §00 was written at 90 km rather than the 160 km the market would prefer. Writing an aspirational range requirement and then assuming a battery that does not exist is the most common failure mode in this vehicle class. The honest concept is range-limited and says so.

07 / SUBSYSTEMS

Where the dragonfly actually shows up

Structure — corrugation, used correctly

The dragonfly wing's pleats are a stiffness solution first and an aerodynamic accident second. ANAX-4 uses sinusoidal corrugated CFRP shear webs in the wing box, canard box and rotor blade spars. Corrugated webs resist shear buckling far better than flat webs of the same mass, which lets the web gauge drop and removes most of the stiffener population. Estimated saving in the wing box: 11–14% versus a conventional flat-web, stiffened design.

What is not copied: the external aerodynamic corrugation. At Re ≈ 5 × 10⁶ a pleated outer surface is simply a drag penalty. The pleats live inside the structure where they belong.

Aeroelasticity — the pterostigma

Whirl flutter is the classic tiltrotor failure mode: the nacelle mass on a flexible pylon couples with the rotor's gyroscopic and aerodynamic moments and goes unstable above a critical airspeed. The dragonfly solved the analogous wing-flutter problem by placing a small dense mass outboard and forward.

ANAX-4 places tuned masses at the outboard-forward corner of each tilt pylon, sized to move the pylon mass axis ahead of the elastic axis and push the whirl-flutter onset above 1.2 × Vdive. Because the mass is discrete and outboard, it buys the margin at roughly a third of the mass cost of stiffening the pylon to the same margin.

Energy and thermal — the abdomen

The boom aft of the cabin carries no lift and no rotor loads. It carries: the 800 V DC bus, twelve isolated battery modules, and a corrugated upper skin acting as a passive radiator. Dragonflies dump thoracic heat down the abdomen; the boom does the same job with a 2.4 m² effective radiating surface that costs no additional drag because the skin is already there. Module isolation and directional overboard venting contain a thermal runaway to one module.

The boom also does the abdomen's other job: a 3.1 m tail moment arm, which shrinks the V-tail to 1.9 m² and saves roughly 18 kg against a short-coupled layout.

Sensing — two eyes and three ocelli

Compound-eye belt

12 × event cameras

Near-omnidirectional coverage with microsecond-scale latency and >120 dB dynamic range — no blooming flying into low sun, no motion blur. Feeds detect-and-avoid.

Small-target motion detection

STMD, dragonfly-derived

Algorithms modelled directly on Odonate target-selective neurons pull small, low-contrast intruders — birds, other UAS — out of moving clutter, which conventional detectors handle badly.

Ocelli triad

3 × wide-FOV horizon sensors

Coarse, fast, absolute attitude from the sky–ground gradient. Independent of the IMU and of GNSS — a dissimilar third channel with a completely different failure signature. This is a certification argument, not just a control one.

Distributed mechanoreception

FBG strain + MEMS pressure taps

Blades and wings instrumented for direct load sensing, feeding gust load alleviation and live stall-margin estimation rather than inferring both from the IMU.

08 / CONTROL

Phase, reinterpreted twice

The dragonfly's single cleverest trick is that it controls the relationship between its front and rear force producers rather than just their magnitude. That idea survives translation, in two separate places.

Use 1 — rotor clocking for acoustics

The coaxial pairs chosen in §04 bring an interaction tone: the lower rotor slices the upper rotor's wake once per blade passage, producing a strong, highly tonal, very annoying signature. The mitigation is active azimuthal phase control between upper and lower rotors, and between the fore and aft stations.

Holding a commanded phase offset detunes the interaction so that the four stations' tones do not reinforce in the far field, and lets the phase schedule be varied with azimuth so that no observer on the ground sits in a persistent constructive-interference lobe. The dragonfly modulates fore–hind stroke phase to manage wake interaction; ANAX-4 modulates rotor index phase to manage wake interaction. Same variable, different mechanism.

Use 2 — sequenced transition

Dragonflies lead with the forewings. ANAX-4 leads with the canard station: the forward nacelles run roughly 8° ahead of the aft nacelles throughout the tilt schedule.

Over-actuation is the point, not a byproduct. With 26 effectors serving 6 degrees of freedom, the allocator has room to trade: it can hold attitude while re-optimising for minimum noise on approach, minimum energy in cruise, or maximum control margin after a fault — without changing the control law. That flexibility is precisely what four independently driven wings buy the dragonfly.

11 / CERTIFICATION

The regulatory path now exists — which changes the design

When eVTOL concepts were first drawn there was no rulebook, and designers could defer the question. That is no longer true, and the framework now constrains real design choices.

  • Airworthiness: FAA powered-lift special class under 14 CFR 21.17(b), with the certification basis established through issue papers and guided by AC 21.17-4. EASA route is SC-VTOL, Enhanced category — required for flight over congested areas, and the source of the 10⁻⁹ target.
  • Pilot qualification: SFAR No. 120 established the powered-lift rating in October 2024.
  • Operations: Part 194, the dedicated powered-lift operating rule, was finalised in March 2026, completing the US regulatory stack alongside Part 135 air carrier requirements.
  • Precedent: Joby received the first US eVTOL type certificate in June 2026, following CAAC certification of EHang's EH216-S and AutoFlight's CarryAll. A certification basis for this class is no longer hypothetical.

The design consequence is concrete: the SC-VTOL Enhanced 10⁻⁹ requirement is what killed the four-single-rotor configuration in §04 and produced the eight-motor architecture. It also drives the dissimilar attitude reference in §07 and the dual-bus split in §09. Three of this aircraft's defining features are regulatory in origin, not aerodynamic.

13 / DISCIPLINE

What was deliberately not copied

A biomimetic design is judged as much by its rejections as its adoptions. Each of these was studied and abandoned for a stated reason.

Flapping wings

Rejected on Reynolds number, wing inertia, and fatigue certification — three independent arguments, §03. This is the whole reason the aircraft looks nothing like a dragonfly.

Aerodynamic corrugation

Only advantageous below Re ≈ 10⁴. The pleats were moved inside the structure, where the stiffness argument still holds at any scale.

Abdominal mass-shift control

Bandwidth orders of magnitude too low, control authority poor, and it puts a moving mass into primary structure. A textbook case of a mechanism that only works because the animal is light.

Membranous, unactuated wing surface

Depends on scale-dependent surface tension, cuticle properties and self-repair mechanisms with no engineering equivalent at 2.3 t. Certifiable primary structure is not negotiable.

Four-wing kinematic mimicry

The topology was kept; the kinematics were not. Copying the arrangement of force producers is engineering. Copying how they move is costume.

Dragonfly-shaped fuselage

A slender segmented body is optimal for an animal that must also perch, mate and moult. A cabin for four people has different requirements. Aesthetic mimicry was excluded from the outset.

The method, in one line

Nothing was adopted because it came from a dragonfly. Each item was adopted because a physical argument survived a change of five orders of magnitude in mass — and the dragonfly was the source of the hypothesis, not the justification.
