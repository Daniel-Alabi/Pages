Our approach

Follow the child. It's not a slogan — it's a method.

Every material on our shelves, every rhythm in our day, exists because a century of observation shows how children actually learn: through their hands, at their own pace, in real community.

✋

Hands before abstraction

Children touch quantity before they write numerals, trace sandpaper letters before they read. Concrete materials make abstract ideas obvious.

🌱

Freedom within limits

Children choose their work and repeat it as long as it serves them. The prepared environment — not the adult — sets the boundaries.

🤝

Mixed-age community

Three-year age spans mean younger children learn by watching, and older children consolidate by teaching. Grace and courtesy are practiced daily, not assigned.

Programs

Three environments, one continuum

Each classroom is prepared for a specific plane of development. Children typically stay with the same guide for the full three-year cycle.

18 months – 3 years

Toddler Community

A calm, language-rich environment where independence begins: real work with real tools, scaled to small hands.

  • Practical life: pouring, dressing, food preparation
  • Toilet learning supported in partnership with families
  • Half-day and full-day schedules
3 – 6 years

Primary (Children's House)

The classic Montessori classroom: sensorial materials, early mathematics, and the explosion into writing and reading.

  • Full sequence of Montessori math & language materials
  • Daily outdoor work in the garden classroom
  • Kindergarten year as the capstone of the cycle
6 – 9 years

Lower Elementary

Cosmic education for the reasoning mind — big stories, research projects, and going-out trips into the community.

  • The Five Great Lessons frame the year
  • Student-planned "going out" excursions
  • Florida-aligned outcomes, Montessori methods
A day at Live Oak

The rhythm of a Primary morning

Predictable rhythm, protected concentration. Here's how a typical day unfolds.

8:00 – 8:30

Arrival & greeting

Each child is greeted by name at the door and settles into the classroom independently.

8:30 – 11:30

Work cycle

Three uninterrupted hours of self-chosen work, individual lessons, and snack at a child's own pace.

11:30 – 12:00

Community time

Songs, grace and courtesy, a story read aloud, observations shared from the morning.

12:00 – 1:00

Lunch & outdoors

Family-style lunch the children help set and clear, then time in the garden and on the trails.

1:00 – 3:00

Rest & afternoon work

Younger children rest; kindergartners return to the classroom for extended lessons.
